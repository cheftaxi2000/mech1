### Slide 13 (p. 12) — Quantum Mechanics Says …
- Handwritten top: «Outcomes of quantum mechanics …»
- Electron motion described by its **wavefunction** (grey: «Function that gives "map" of where electron is, given constraints of Uncertainty Principle»).
- Due to the Uncertainty Principle we only know probabilities (grey: «For example, the probability that the electron is in a certain region»).
- Bohr orbits become **atomic orbitals**.
- Orbitals described by four numbers: n, ℓ, m_ℓ, m_s.
- Principal quantum number, n ⇒ 1, 2, 3, … (grey: «Same n as in the Bohr model»); n called «shell» ⇒ size of orbital.

### Slide 14 (p. 13) — Quantum Mechanics Says …
- Orbitals described by four numbers: n, ℓ, m_ℓ, m_s.
- Angular quantum number, ℓ ⇒ integers between 0 and n − 1. Note: range of ℓ depends on n (shell). ℓ called «subshell» ⇒ shape of orbital. Table: ℓ = 0, 1, 2, 3 ↔ s, p, d, f. Handwritten: «By convention we use letters for ℓ. s = "sharp", p = "principal", d = "diffuse", f = "fundamental". These letters come from early days describing atomic emission lines.»
- Magnetic quantum number, m_ℓ ⇒ −ℓ, −ℓ+1, …, 0, …, ℓ−1, ℓ. Note: range of m_ℓ depends on ℓ (subshell). m_ℓ relates to orientation of orbital in space.

### Slides 20–21 (pp. 19–20) — Last Quantum Number?

(Slide 21 adds: «And only two possible spin orientations»; picture with a toy figure: «What is spin, exactly? — It's like when a ball spins but it's not a ball. — And it doesn't spin.»; red: «Outcome of quantum mechanics».)

### Slide 22 (p. 21) — Energy of Atomic Orbitals in Multielectron Atoms?

## Exercise class 6 (pages used)
- p. 4 Exercise 2: electron of hydrogen excited from n = 1 to n = ∞; ΔE = 218 · 10⁻²⁰ J/atom; ΔE = hc/λ; λ = hc/ΔE = 9,11227 · 10⁻⁸ m = 91,1 nm → ultraviolet.
- p. 5 «6.4 Quantum Mechanics»: Heisenberg: Δx · Δp ≥ h/4π; atomic orbitals: regions where we have 90 % chance of finding the electron; each dot symbolises a potential electron position; new atomic model: electrons occupy different shells around the nucleus; each shell contains subshells (s, p, d, f), which are groups of orbitals; each orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons. Exercise 3: «In a contour representation of an orbital, … the electron is confined to move about the nucleus on the outer surface of the shape.» TRUE/FALSE → FALSE: «90 % probability that the electron is inside the surface».
- p. 6: quantum numbers: 1) principal n: shell, size of orbital, n = {1, 2, 3, …}; 2) angular ℓ: subshell, shape of orbital, ℓ = {0, 1, 2, 3, …, n−1} = {s, p, d, f, g, …}; 3) magnetic m_ℓ: orientation of orbital in space, m_ℓ = {−ℓ, −ℓ+1, …, ℓ−1, ℓ}; 4) spin magnetic m_s: the two electrons in an orbital are not the same, m_s distinguishes them, m_s = {−1/2, 1/2}. Table 6.2 and picture of s (2 e⁻), p (6 e⁻), d (10 e⁻), f (14 e⁻). Pauli's exclusion principle: each electron in an atom must have a unique set of quantum numbers (an s and another p orbital intersect!).
- p. 7 «6.5 Electron configuration»: each element has a unique way of how electrons are distributed across the orbitals; use the periodic table to determine in which order to fill orbitals; notation n·ℓ^(# e⁻ in subshell): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p⁶ 5s² 4d¹⁰ 5p⁶ 6s² 4f¹⁴ … until all electrons are filled in. Exercise 4: a) H: 1s¹ (annotated: shell, subshell, # e⁻ in subshell); b) He: 1s²; c) Li: 1s² 2s¹; d) C: 1s² 2s² 2p²; e) Se: [Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p⁴; f) Mg: [Ne] 3s². «The further down you go in the PT, the longer it gets; condensed notation: plug in the noble gas from one row up.»
- p. 8: Hund's rule: every orbital in a subshell is singly filled with electrons that have the same spin before an orbital is doubly occupied. Exercise 5 (together): for oxygen, draw orbitals as boxes that can hold 2 e⁻: 1) draw all relevant orbitals (1s, 2s, 2p ×3); 2) fill the 1s and 2s subshells; 3) singly fill the 2p subshell, same spin; 4) doubly fill the remaining e⁻. «Unpaired electrons: e⁻ of singly filled orbitals.» Energies: for subshells in the same shell (same n) E_s < E_p < E_d < E_f; this is because the distance of an average electron in a subshell rises: Δx_s < Δx_p < Δx_d < Δx_f; the reason for filling the 4s before the 3d orbital is because the potential energy of 4s is lower (diagram: 3d crossing «this before that»).
- p. 9 Exercise 6 (drawing of a d_xy orbital, from Brown): (a) smallest value of n: n ≥ 3 («d only in shells above …»), (b) ℓ = 2 → d, (c) largest possible m_ℓ: m_ℓ = {−2, −1, 0, 1, 2} → 2. Exercise 7: identify the group for the generalised configurations and give the number of unpaired electrons: (a) [noble gas] ns² np⁵ → F or Cl, 1 unpaired e⁻; (b) [noble gas] ns² (n−1)d² → Ti or Zr, 2; (c) [noble gas] ns² (n−1)d¹⁰ np¹ → Ga or In, 1; (d) [noble gas] ns² (n−2)f⁶ → Sm or Pu, 6 unpaired e⁻.
- Exercise 6's p. 3 («6.3 Bohr Model»): E_n = −h·c·R_H / n², R_H = 1,097 · 10⁷ m⁻¹ (Rydberg constant); ΔE_{i→f} = hcR_H (1/n_i² − 1/n_f²); ΔE > 0: e⁻ moved outwards, photon absorbed; ΔE < 0: e⁻ moved inwards, photon released.
