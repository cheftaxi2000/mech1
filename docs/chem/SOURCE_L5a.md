## Slide 7 — «Thermodynamics II»
Last time we discussed energy conservation. Today: which processes will occur «spontaneously»?
- «Spontaneous process?» — process that occurs without assistance. (Rand) «Based on our experiences».
- «Criterion?» — Spontaneous processes seem to favor lowering their energy. Ex: at constant P, is process spontaneous if ΔH < 0? (exothermic)
- «Cannot be complete answer!» — Many spontaneous processes do have ΔH < 0 (Rand: «Ex: H₂ combustion»). But others have ΔH > 0 (endothermic). Ex: ice melting is spontaneous, ΔH = +6.0 kJ/mol.
- (red) «Something is missing in our description … What?» (Rand above) «If ΔH < 0 is not the criterion for spontaneity, then which? First, we need to define some terms…»

## Slide 8 — «Spontaneous versus Nonspontaneous Processes»
«Before answering that question, let's describe characteristics of spontaneous processes.»
- If process is spontaneous, reverse process is nonspontaneous. Ball does not spontaneously roll up hill.
- Experimental conditions (e.g., T, P) are important for determining spontaneity. Ex: ice melts at +1 °C, but not at −1 °C.
- Spontaneous does not mean fast! Just means process will occur. (grey) How fast? Answered by kinetics: Lectures 10–11.
- Nonspontaneous does not mean impossible! May be possible process if we add energy. Nonspontaneous just means it will not occur without our help.
- (red) «We also need to define …»

## Slide 9 — «Reversible versus Irreversible Processes»
- «Reversible process?» Process can be reversed with no change to surroundings. «“Reversed” means system is returned to its initial state».
- «Irreversible process?» Surroundings are changed when process is reversed.
- «Let's discuss these definitions with example of heat flow …» We know from everyday life, hot-to-cold heat transfer is spontaneous. Cold-to-hot heat transfer is not spontaneous. «Energy tends to spread from concentrated to less concentrated».

## Slide 10 — «Example: Heat Transfer»
Figure: a blue box (surroundings, «T_cold») containing a red box («System», «T_hot»); small red arrows leave the system.
- Heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold. Reversible heat transfer is ideal case: T_hot = T + δT, T_cold = T; δT ≡ infinitesimal temperature.
- To reverse process, change T_hot → T − δT. Heat flow reverses without changing surroundings.
- «Reversible process?» Process that reverses direction after infinitesimal change to property of system. (grey) In above example, T was changed from T + δT to T − δT.

## Slide 11 — «Another Example: Isothermal Expansion»
«Expand ideal gas into vacuum at constant temperature.» Upper drawing: piston on the left, «Vacuum» chamber, a blue «partition», gas (red dots) in the right chamber; «remove partition», w = 0; after: gas spread through the whole cylinder. Label «spontaneous irreversible».
- How could we reverse by some infinitesimal change to property of system? To return system to initial state, surroundings need to do work on gas to compress it. Thus, surroundings have changed.
- «Alternative:» lower drawing: gas in cylinder with piston held by pressure P − δP; «reduce pressure infinitesimally» → gas expands against the piston: «spontaneous reversible». If we increase P to P + δP at any time, process is reversed. Reversible process is idealized process.

## Slide 12 — «Real Processes»
We can never achieve idealized reversible processes in practice.
- All real processes are irreversible. All spontaneous processes are real processes. ∴ **All spontaneous processes are irreversible** (highlighted).
- To return system to initial state: requires work (w > 0).
- While E is conserved during spontaneous process, E tends to spread out and become less useful. (grey, «1st Law») «The spreading of energy makes some of it unavailable to do work».
- (Rand under) «However, ideal reversible processes are helpful for defining another state function that quantifies this tendency of energy to spread and become less useful…»

## Slide 13 — «Entropy, S»
- «A measure of the tendency for E to spread, which reduces system's ability to do work.» Also related to randomness or disorder in system.
- S is another state function ⇒ ΔS = S_final − S_initial. (Rand: «Of system»; «Path doesn't matter».)
- For isothermal process (constant T): ΔS = q_rev / T, with q_rev ≡ heat flow for reversible process and T ≡ absolute temperature.
- (Rand below) «Note: Only one path is associated with q_rev ⇒ unique! Even if process is irreversible, because S is a state function, we can use idealized reversible process to determine ΔS.»

## Slide 14 — «ΔS for Phase Changes»
Melting and boiling in isothermal processes. Photo: ice cubes at 0 °C.
- Ex: What is ΔS if ice melts at T = 0 °C and P = 101.3 kPa? ΔH_fus = 6.0 × 10³ J·mol⁻¹ (Rand: «Heat of melting for H₂O»); q = ΔH_fus at constant P.
- Imagine melting slowly at T_surr = 0 °C + δT (Rand: «T of surroundings infinitesimally warmer») ⇒ process is reversible; q_rev = ΔH_fus = 6.0 × 10³ J·mol⁻¹ (Rand: «Endothermic process»).
- ΔS = q_rev/T = (6.0×10³ / 273) J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ = +22 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹. Note: need to use absolute temperature.

## Slide 15 — «“1st Law Equivalent” for Entropy?»
(Rand above) «So S is a state function like E, but is it conserved like E? In other words, what is the 1st-Law equivalent for S?»
- 1st Law: E is conserved. Is S also conserved? Consider ice cube melting in your hand: Process is irreversible! Ice cube (0 °C) ≡ system; surroundings ≡ hand (37 °C). (Rand) «Ice cube (system)», «Your hand (surroundings)».
- ΔS_sys = q_rev/T = (6.0×10³/273) J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ = +22 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹; ΔS_surr = (−6.0×10³/310) J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ = −19 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹.
- Thus, total ΔS for universe due to this irreversible process: ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr = +3.0 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ — «∴ ΔS_univ has increased!» (Rand) «Note: If T_surr = 273 K + δT and process is reversible, then ΔS_univ = 0».
- **Slide arithmetic:** 6000/273 = 21.98 and 6000/310 = 19.35 give ΔS_univ = +2.6 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹; the slide's +3.0 comes from subtracting the rounded values 22 − 19.

## Slide 16 — «2nd Law of Thermodynamics»
(Rand above) «Thus, a thermodynamic law exists for entropy, but entropy is not conserved in general.» «Entropy is not conserved in general.»
- Reversible process: ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr = 0. Irreversible process: ΔS_univ = ΔS_sys + ΔS_surr > 0. Both together: «Criterion for spontaneity». (Rand) «However, we will determine more useful criterion below».
- Since all real processes are irreversible …: «Entropy of universe increases for any spontaneous process» (2nd Law, Rand).
- Notation: ΔS_sys → ΔS because we care about ΔS_sys.

## Slide 17 — «Entropy: Molecular Interpretation»
(Rand above) «We can imagine that if we have 1 mol of molecules (6×10²³ molecules), many possible arrangements (microstates) consistent with the macroscopic state of the system exist. This leads to a microscopic (molecular) description of entropy, S.»
- Kinetic-molecular theory of gases (Rand: «Lecture #2»): molecules moving with statistical distribution; explains spontaneous expansion of gas into vacuum. Figure: box of blue and red dots; Maxwell–Boltzmann curves at 0 °C and 100 °C (fraction of molecules vs. molecular speed, m/s).
- «Statistical thermodynamics»: connects macroscopic state of system (Rand: described by V, T, etc.) to microscopic arrangement of molecules. «Microstate»: one particular arrangement of the molecules (Rand: consistent with macroscopic state; «That is, the positions, velocities, and directions for all molecules in system»).

## Slide 18 — «Boltzmann's Equation»
(Rand: «Ludwig Boltzmann (Austrian physicist, 1844–1906)»)
- **S = k_B ln W**; ln ≡ natural logarithm. W ≡ number of microstates consistent with specific macroscopic state. k_B ≡ Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K.
- • Entropy measures number of microstates consistent with macroscopic state. • Entropy of system increases with number of microstates.
- ΔS = k_B ln W_final − k_B ln W_initial = k_B ln (W_final / W_initial). (Rand: «Using log identity: ln A − ln B = ln(A/B)»)
- (Rand below) «Note: R: Gas constant from PV = nRT is related to k_B. R = k_B·N_A = 1.38×10⁻²³ J·K⁻¹ · 6.02×10²³ mol⁻¹ = 8.31 J·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹»; «k_B → energy per molecule per Kelvin; R → energy per mole per Kelvin».

## Slide 19 — «Implications?»
In general, W and S increase with … «… increases in»: • system's V • system's T • number of molecules • complexity of molecules; «… and also with»: • melting of solids • vaporizing of liquids. Framed: «Entropy: Measure of spread in energy or randomness/disorder in system» (Rand: «Spread in energy increases ways that energy can be distributed among the molecules in the system»). Figure: box of dots. (Rand below) «Increasing complexity in molecule increases its “degrees of freedom” or forms of motion for each molecule. More degrees of freedom means more possible microstates».

## Slide 20 — «Engineers: Entropy Warriors»
Images from Doctor Who («Logopolis», radio telescope and a group of figures). Text: Engineers spend large amounts of energy to fight the 2nd Law of Thermodynamics. We make highly ordered, useful materials. Otherwise, universe increases in disorder. While ordering our small part of the universe, we must increase disorder somewhere else by more!

## Slide 21 — «3rd Law of Thermodynamics»
(Rand) «Finally». As we cool any system, it has less energy, less degrees of freedom, and less microstates. **3rd Law:** A pure, perfect crystalline solid substance at T = 0 K has S = 0. It has only one microstate, i.e., W = 1.
