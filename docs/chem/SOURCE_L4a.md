## Slide 7 — «Thermodynamics I»

## Slide 8 — «Energy»
- Two types: «kinetic energy» (motion) and «potential energy» (stored energy). In chemistry, potential energy can be stored in chemical bonds. To understand thermodynamics, we must quantify energy…
- Start with conservation of energy: «1st Law of Thermodynamics»: energy can be converted between different types, but it cannot be created or destroyed.
- We will distinguish between … «system» (reactants and products) and «surroundings» (everything else).
- (Rand) «Since thermodynamics is “study of energy and its transformations”»; «See tutorial in exercise #1».

## Slide 9 — «System Types»
- Open: matter and energy can be exchanged with surroundings. Photo: boiling pot. (Rand: «Ex: boiling pot»)
- Closed: energy can be exchanged with surroundings (but not matter). Figure: piston–cylinder with molecules. (Rand: «Ex: H₂ combustion piston»)
- Isolated: neither matter nor energy is exchanged with surroundings. Photo: thermos flask. (Rand: «Ex: sealed, thermally insulated flask»)

## Slide 10 — «Total Energy for System»
- Internal Energy, E: total kinetic plus potential energy in system. Typically, we do not know the actual value of E. Instead, we focus on changes in E:
  ΔE = E_final − E_initial. After and before «something» happened to system. Usually, a chemical reaction.
- (Rand) «We need to define the total energy of our system».

## Slide 11 — «Energy Changes»
- ΔE = E_final − E_initial. «Sign is important!» ΔE > 0: system has gained energy (Rand: «From surroundings»). ΔE < 0: system has lost energy (Rand: «To surroundings»).
- For a chemical reaction, E_final is for products and E_initial for reactants.
- «Energy Diagram»: vertical axis E; level E_initial = «2 H₂(g) + O₂(g)» (reactants); red arrow downward «reaction», ΔE < 0; level E_final = «2 H₂O(l)» (products).
- Ex: 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2 H₂O(l); «Energy released to surroundings».

## Slide 12 — «1st Law Revisited»
- «As energy cannot be created or destroyed, it must go somewhere!»
- ΔE = q + w, with q ≡ heat added to system, w ≡ work done on system.
- Recall that w = F·d (work is a force applied over a distance). So pushing a piston does work!
- «Sign convention»: ⊕ means E of our system has increased. q > 0: heat added to system; q < 0: heat removed from system; w > 0: work done on system; w < 0: work done by system.
- (Rand) «Unfortunately, this is not the only sign convention for 1st law. See 3rd semester Thermodynamics in D-MAVT.»

## Slide 13 — «Heat and Work: Sign Convention»
Three drawings of a safe containing a bag «Internal energy»: left «System is interior of vault»; middle: arrows into the safe «Heat gained q > 0», «Work done on system w > 0», caption «Energy deposited into system, ΔE > 0»; right: arrows out «Heat lost q < 0», «Work done by system w < 0», caption «Energy withdrawn from system, ΔE < 0». (Rand) «For this course». Bottom note: «We are following convention in Brown».

## Slide 14 — «Endothermic and Exothermic Processes»; «State Functions»
- Endothermic: q > 0 → heat absorbed by system. Ex: ice melting.
- Exothermic: q < 0 → heat released by system. Ex: H₂ combustion.
- State Functions: internal energy E is an example of a state function. The value of a state function only depends on the current «state» of the system, NOT the path the system took to get there. The current state is specified by conditions: P, V, … E also depends on the quantity of matter in the system; (grey) «E is extensive property».

## Slide 15 — «E is State Function»
- ΔE = E_final − E_initial only depends on initial and final state. We do not need to worry about how we did it. In contrast, q and w are not state functions → «path matters!»
- Ex: 2 H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2 H₂O(l): «Combust 2 moles of hydrogen».
  Method 1: in burner that produces all heat: ΔE = q. Method 2: in piston that produces work + heat: ΔE = q + w.
  «ΔE is same, but q and w depend on method.» (blue) «What other state functions can we consider?» (red). Photos: flame, piston with molecules.
